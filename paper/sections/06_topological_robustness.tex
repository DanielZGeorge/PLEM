\section{Analysis and Design for Topological Robustness}
\label{sec:topological}

\subsection{The Connectivity Blind Spot}
\label{subsec:blindspot}

Pointwise EDT tolerance matching is not ``connectivity-injective.'' Concretely (proof sketch; the
full statement is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}):

\begin{quote}
For a road mask $G_c$ of width $w$ and tolerance $d_c$, there exist predicted masks $P_c$ obtained
from $G_c$ by deleting an arbitrarily large fraction of pixels, including masks in which the
connected skeleton of $G_c$ is broken into many isolated fragments, such that
$\mathrm{DTAF1}_c(P_c, G_c) = 1.0$, provided the surviving pixels remain within $d_c$ of every
ground-truth pixel.
\end{quote}

The road-breakage sweep of Section~\ref{subsec:motivation-results} is the empirical witness: DTAF1
stays at exactly $1.000$ through 90\% random dropout on the synthetic scene.

\subsection{APLS as Connectivity Recovery, and Its Sensitivity to Fragmentation}
\label{subsec:apls-recovery}

APLS (Section~\ref{subsec:apls}) recovers the signal DTAF1 lacks. Its score depends on the length of
the shortest path between control points, not only on how close points are to each other, so it is
sensitive to whether a path exists at all. Against dropout fraction, the empirical curve has two
regimes: an almost flat, low-sensitivity regime while the skeleton is still
mostly connected, followed by a sharp drop once fragmentation sets in
(Table~\ref{tab:breakage-topo}).

\begin{table}
  \caption{Road-breakage sweep: DTAF1 vs.\ CBHM vs.\ APLS vs.\ DTAF1-Topo.}
  \label{tab:breakage-topo}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Dropout & \texttt{dtaf1} & \texttt{cbhm} & \texttt{road\_apls} & \texttt{dtaf1\_topo} \\
    \midrule
    0.0 & 1.000 & 1.000 & 1.000 & 1.000 \\
    0.1 & 1.000 & 0.965 & 0.898 & 0.946 \\
    0.2 & 1.000 & 0.919 & 0.883 & 0.938 \\
    0.3 & 1.000 & 0.909 & \textbf{0.247} & \textbf{0.397} \\
    0.5 & 1.000 & 0.805 & 0.029 & 0.057 \\
    0.7 & 1.000 & 0.606 & 0.005 & 0.010 \\
    1.0 & 0.500 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The transition happens between 0.2 and 0.3, where \texttt{road\_apls} falls from $0.883$ to $0.247$
in a single step. At that point the largest connected fragment of the road skeleton detaches from
enough control-point pairs that shortest paths start to disappear instead of only getting longer.
\texttt{dtaf1} shows no similar transition anywhere in the swept range.

\subsection{Harmonic-Blending Design for DTAF1-Topo}
\label{subsec:dtaf1topo}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Blending primitive, \texttt{metrics/dtaf1\_topo.py:29-31,74-87}}]
def _harmonic_mean(a, b):
    denom = a + b
    return float(2 * a * b / denom) if denom > 0 else 0.0

# per class in linear_classes:
entry["blended"] = _harmonic_mean(base["f1"], a)  # a = apls
# non-linear classes keep entry["blended"] = base["f1"]
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Closed-Form Boundary Behavior}

The harmonic mean of $F1$ and $\mathrm{APLS}$ has a simple shape at its boundaries that can be
analyzed exactly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathrm{APLS}=0, F1=1$ (the worst case of DTAF1's blind spot): $\mathrm{blended}=0$. A road
    that matches perfectly within tolerance but is fully disconnected scores exactly zero once
    blended, however high $F1$ is.
  \item $\mathrm{APLS}=1, F1=1$: $\mathrm{blended}=1$, which leaves DTAF1's perfect score
    unchanged.
  \item For fixed $F1=1$, $\mathrm{blended}(\mathrm{APLS}) = 2\mathrm{APLS}/(1+\mathrm{APLS})$,
    which is concave and always at least $\mathrm{APLS}$. Since the harmonic mean never exceeds
    the larger of its two inputs, the blend can only pull a high $F1$ down toward the generally
    lower APLS score and never raises it above $F1$. This asymmetry is what closes the blind spot.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Numerical Validation via the Road-Breakage Sweep}

Where along the dropout axis the blend departs from plain DTAF1 has no closed form.
Table~\ref{tab:breakage-topo} shows \texttt{dtaf1\_topo} tracking \texttt{road\_apls} closely, with
both driven close to zero by dropout fractions of 0.5--0.7, while \texttt{dtaf1} stays at $1.000$
throughout; \texttt{tests/test\_metrics\_sanity.py::\allowbreak{}TestRoadBreakageRegression} asserts
this. The same pattern holds on 50 real SpaceNet tiles (Table~\ref{tab:realdata-breakage}).
