\section{Analysis and Design for Composite Robustness}
\label{sec:composite}

\subsection{Class-Imbalance--Controlled Tasks}
\label{subsec:class-imbalance}

\texttt{sweep\_class\_imbalance} and \texttt{sweep\_sparse\_class\_offset} define a controlled axis
of task complexity: the road's share of ground-truth pixels (0.4--3.1\% in \texttt{sweep\_class\_imbalance}, with the road missing entirely
from the prediction) or its offset (\texttt{sweep\_sparse\_class\_offset}, with the road share
fixed at ${\sim}0.4\%$). In both sweeps the harsh harmonic \texttt{cbhm} sits at exactly $0.000$
for every non-trivial perturbation level, while \texttt{cbhm\_soft} follows the dominant building
class and stays between $0.758$ and $1.000$ over the same ranges. The \emph{harsh-composite
collapse threshold}, the smallest perturbation at which \texttt{cbhm} first reaches exactly 0, is
reached immediately: once one class is fully wrong, the harmonic mean has no tolerance, however
small that class's share of the scene.

\subsection{Weighted Reduction as an Efficient Corrective Proxy}
\label{subsec:weighted-reduction}

\begin{align}
  \mathrm{cbhm\_soft} = w_{\mathrm{linear}} \cdot \mathrm{clDice}_{\mathrm{mean,weighted}} +
                         w_{\mathrm{polygon}} \cdot \mathrm{BF}_{\mathrm{mean,weighted}}
\end{align}
where $w_{\mathrm{linear}} = N_{\mathrm{linear}} / (N_{\mathrm{linear}} + N_{\mathrm{polygon}})$
is each type's share of the total ground-truth pixels (0.5/0.5 if both totals are 0).

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, float=tp, caption={Type weights and \texttt{cbhm\_soft}, \texttt{metrics/unified.py:109-116}}]
type_total = cl_total + bf_total
w_cl = float(cl_total / type_total) if type_total > 0 else 0.5
w_bf = float(bf_total / type_total) if type_total > 0 else 0.5
cbhm_soft = w_cl * cl_mean_weighted + w_bf * bf_mean_weighted
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{cbhm\_soft} reweights per-class scores that DTAF1 and CBHM already compute, so it costs
nothing extra and leaves the harmonic-mean composite itself unchanged. It uses an arithmetic mean,
since a geometric mean also collapses to exactly 0 whenever one input is 0
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, Sections~\ref{app:a3}--\ref{app:a4}).

\textbf{Worked example.} The real failure that motivated this fix came from SpaceNet imagery
(\texttt{composite\_vs\_submetric\_report.ipynb}, tile \texttt{Khartoum\_img371}). A 12px road
offset gave $\mathrm{cbhm}=0.000$ despite $\mathrm{dtaf1}=0.934$ (per-class road F1 $=0.867$,
precision $=0.872$, recall $=0.863$). The road covers only ${\sim}0.1\%$ of the image's pixels, and
clDice collapsed entirely on this sparse, offset road, which dragged the harmonic mean to exactly
zero even though the dominant building class was essentially perfect. \texttt{cbhm\_soft} recovers
only partially here ($0.000 \to 0.285$), because the road has more ground-truth pixels than the
building despite its tiny share of the image area, and area-weighting only helps when the failing
class is also the minority by pixel count. On \texttt{Khartoum\_img333}, where the building does
dominate by pixel count, the recovery is clean: $\mathrm{cbhm}$ $0.48$--$0.52 \to
\mathrm{cbhm\_soft}$ $0.85$. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to this limitation.

\subsection{Choosing Per-Class Tolerance Radii}
\label{subsec:tolerance-radii}

We validated the default tolerances, 10\,px for roads and 2\,px for buildings
(\texttt{DEFAULT\_TOLERANCES}), against the offset and erosion sweeps of Section~\ref{subsec:motivation-results}. The road offset sweep shows
DTAF1 holding at $1.000$ up to exactly the 10px boundary and softening immediately past it, so the
tolerance neither rejects correct predictions nor lets everything pass. The sweep is a cheap
stand-in for re-annotating real data at every candidate tolerance. PLEM's tolerances are fixed,
physically motivated constants ($d = \text{physical\_metres} / \text{GSD\_metres\_per\_pixel}$),
not learned ones; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} lists learned or adaptive radii as future work.
