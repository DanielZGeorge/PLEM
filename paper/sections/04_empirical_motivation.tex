\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:motivation-results}

Table~\ref{tab:failure-modes} comes from a direct run of the library on this scene. Four
single-axis sweeps add to it:

\textbf{Road offset sweep} (tolerance $=10$px). Road IoU and clDice both collapse to $0.000$ as
soon as the offset reaches 4px, while DTAF1 stays at $1.000$ up to an offset of 10px, exactly its
tolerance boundary, and then degrades to $0.667$ at 12px and $0.500$ from 16px onward, where the
still-perfect building class holds up the macro average.

\textbf{Road breakage sweep} (seed 42). Road IoU degrades roughly linearly with the dropout
fraction ($1.000 \to 0.500$ at 50\% $\to 0.201$ at 80\%) and clDice degrades faster and less
evenly, but DTAF1 stays at exactly $1.000$ for every dropout fraction from 0\% through 90\%, and
only drops to $0.500$ once all road pixels are gone, so it cannot tell an intact road from one with
90\% of its pixels randomly deleted (Section~\ref{subsec:motivation-interpretation} explains why).

\textbf{Road thickness sweep} (ground truth fixed at 3px). Road IoU falls from $1.000$ at the
correct thickness to $0.157$ at a predicted thickness of 21px, while clDice and DTAF1 both stay at
$1.000$ up to 13px and only start to soften at extreme over-thickness, so their width insensitivity
holds over a wide practical range.

\textbf{Building erosion sweep}. All three metrics degrade together, roughly in proportion to the
erosion radius (building IoU $1.000{\to}0.203$, BF $1.000{\to}0.512$, DTAF1 $1.000{\to}0.728$ at
radius 11). This is the one scenario in which IoU's verdict was never wrong, and we include it as a
control.

\subsection{Interpretation via Distance-Transform Geometry}
\label{subsec:motivation-interpretation}

DTAF1's tolerance radius $d_c$ defines a disc of admissible positional error around every pixel: a
predicted pixel counts as correct if any ground-truth pixel of the same class lies within $d_c$,
and the same holds the other way round for recall. This is why width and small-offset errors are
absorbed, since every pixel of an over-thick or slightly shifted road still has a true road pixel
within $d_c$, so precision and recall both stay near 1. The same geometry is also why DTAF1 is
blind to breakage. The tolerance radius has no notion of which surviving pixels are connected to
each other; it only asks whether there is a pixel of the same class nearby, a purely local,
per-pixel question. Random dropout removes pixels roughly uniformly, so even at high dropout
fractions the remaining pixels still tend to lie within $d_c$ of most ground-truth locations, as a
spatial Poisson-thinning argument predicts: for a road of width $w$ and tolerance $d_c \gg w$, the
probability that some surviving pixel lies within $d_c$ of a given ground-truth pixel stays high
until the surviving fraction becomes small relative to $w/d_c$. clDice does carry a global
connectivity signal through skeletonization, which is why it degrades faster and less evenly than
DTAF1 under the same sweep, but its zero tolerance to any positional offset rules it out as a
replacement for DTAF1. A tolerance radius has to be wide enough to absorb position and width noise,
and at that radius it is necessarily blind to connectivity. This is the structural reason we add a
separate connectivity term (APLS, Section~\ref{subsec:apls}) instead of shrinking $d_c$, which
Section~\ref{sec:topological} develops formally.
