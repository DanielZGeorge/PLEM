\subsection{Real-Data Case Studies}
\label{subsec:realdata-results}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finding 1: DTAF1's blind spot on real data.} Averaged over 24 real SpaceNet tiles
    (Vegas and Khartoum), deleting 75\% of road pixels leaves plain DTAF1 at $1.000$, while CBHM
    collapses to ${\sim}0.57$ as \texttt{cldice\_mean} falls to ${\sim}0.40$.
    \texttt{dtaf1\_weighted} does not fix this and also stays at 1.000, since area-weighting only
    changes how per-class scores are combined, not the per-pixel tolerance matching within a
    class. \texttt{dtaf1\_topo} has not yet been run on this sample
    (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item \textbf{Finding 2: CBHM's sparse-class collapse} (Section~\ref{subsec:weighted-reduction}).
    On \texttt{Khartoum\_img371} a 12px road offset gives $\mathrm{cbhm}=0.000$ against
    $\mathrm{dtaf1}=0.934$, and \texttt{cbhm\_soft} recovers only to $0.285$. On
    \texttt{Khartoum\_img333}, where the building dominates by pixel count, it recovers cleanly
    ($\mathrm{cbhm}$ $0.48$--$0.52 \to \mathrm{cbhm\_soft}$ $0.85$).
  \item \textbf{Aggregate over all 24 tiles.} \texttt{road\_offset} at 16px moves
    $\mathrm{cbhm}\ 0.669 \to \mathrm{cbhm\_soft}\ 0.712$, and \texttt{road\_dropout} at 0.75 moves
    $\mathrm{cbhm}\ 0.568 \to \mathrm{cbhm\_soft}\ 0.646$.
  \item \textbf{Point features on 15 Potsdam crops.} Under \texttt{point\_dropout}, precision stays
    flat at 1.000 while recall falls linearly to 0. Under \texttt{point\_clutter}, recall stays
    flat at 1.000 while precision falls $1.0 \to 0.36 \to 0.13$ at 5 and 20 spurious blobs. Under
    \texttt{point\_jitter}, the score drops to ${\sim}0.38$ by 6px on real data, faster than in the
    synthetic sweep, which we attribute to real objects being packed closer together.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Loss Ablation Results}
\label{subsec:lossablation-results}

The seven sweeps of Section~\ref{subsec:lossablation-setup} confirm the qualitative picture that
Table~\ref{tab:correspondence} predicts. Each new loss term responds to the perturbation family its
\texttt{metrics/} counterpart is designed to catch, and stays flat as a negative control under
perturbations that do not touch its configured classes. The values below are each term's own raw
loss at representative severities, not the cumulative \texttt{plus\_X} values.

\textbf{Road offset sweep} (tolerance radius $=10$px). The \texttt{tolerance} term stays almost flat
($0.0043$) up to an offset of 10px and then climbs ($0.0056$ at 12px), exactly at its configured
tolerance boundary, as DTAF1 does. The \texttt{cldice} term jumps almost at once (already a loss delta of
$1.0$ by an offset of 2px) and then plateaus at its collapsed value, which reproduces clDice's
documented lack of tolerance to offset.

\textbf{Road thickness sweep} (ground truth fixed at 3px). The \texttt{cldice} term tracks the
\texttt{ce\_dice} baseline closely up to a thickness of 13px, staying within ${\sim}0.05$ of it at
every point up to a $4\times$ width ratio, and then diverges sharply from 15px onward, consistent
with the $3\times$ unit test of Section~\ref{subsec:cldice-loss}.

\textbf{Building erosion sweep}. The \texttt{tolerance} term tracks building erosion closely, with
a consistent gap above \texttt{ce\_dice} (for example $1.2338$ against $1.0526$ at an erosion radius
of 11px) that reflects the tight 2px building tolerance. The \texttt{cldice} term stays almost
identical to \texttt{ce\_dice} throughout ($1.0531$ against $1.0526$), the expected negative control,
since \texttt{SoftClDiceLoss} is configured over linear classes only.

\textbf{Point jitter, dropout and clutter sweeps}. \texttt{heatmap} is by far the most
shape-sensitive term, rising from a baseline of $0.58$ to $70.6$ by 8px of jitter and then
plateauing or declining slightly as points scatter further from any plausible match.
\texttt{tolerance} and \texttt{cldice} stay flat at their baseline values throughout every point
sweep, as negative controls should. \texttt{ce\_dice} also rises modestly under point perturbations
($0.51 \to 0.84$ under jitter), but much less sharply than \texttt{heatmap}, and without
saturating.

The module docstring of \texttt{tests/test\_loss\_sensitivity.py} holds the full numeric tables.

\texttt{dtaf1\_topo} reads well below \texttt{dtaf1} on SpaceNet (0.482 against 0.771,
Table~\ref{tab:scaled-test}). This is its first use on real data, and the gap is APLS penalizing

\textbf{Road intersections.} On the SpaceNet test tiles, 82 of 83 of which contain at least one
intersection, the model predicts 547 intersections against 1,389 real ones, and 270 match within
3\,m, for a micro precision of 0.494, recall of 0.194 and F1 of 0.279 (mean per-tile F1 0.213). The
validation tiles chose a threshold of 0.1 (validation micro F1 0.280); 0.07 was almost tied (0.278),
with a predicted-per-real ratio of 1.19 instead of 0.38. Two diagnostics bound what calibration
alone can achieve. For \emph{separability}, the head's probability at real intersections has a
median of 0.046 (10th--90th percentile 0.016--0.120), against a median of 0.013 (90th percentile
0.029, 99th 0.052) on road pixels more than 30\,px from any intersection, so the head ranks
junctions above plain road but the distributions overlap. For the \emph{recall ceiling}, even at a
threshold of 0.002 only 62\% of validation intersections have any peak within 10\,px, so a third of
junctions are invisible to the head at any threshold.

\textbf{Metric claim: supported.} On SpaceNet the conventional metrics call the two models a tie.
The paired mIoU difference is $+0.002$ (95\% CI $-0.004$ to $+0.008$, $p = 0.64$), and pixel
accuracy is 0.003 lower for the joint model. Every PLEM metric separates them, with confidence
intervals that exclude zero: \texttt{dtaf1} $+0.069$, clDice $+0.043$, \texttt{cbhm} $+0.043$,
\texttt{dtaf1\_topo} $+0.038$, \texttt{cbhm\_soft} $+0.038$, road APLS $+0.020$ and BF $+0.020$
($p \le 0.002$ for each). The tie in mIoU hides two opposite moves, road IoU $+0.036$ and building
IoU $-0.026$. On buildings, IoU and BF point in opposite directions: the joint model's footprints
overlap the ground truth less but have better-placed boundaries. Tile by tile, the mIoU and
\texttt{dtaf1\_topo} differences have opposite signs on 30 of 83 tiles (23 of 83 for
\texttt{cbhm}), and the largest case, \texttt{Vegas\_img1407}, loses 0.088 mIoU while gaining 0.109
\texttt{dtaf1\_topo} (APLS $+0.20$). Within either model, Kendall's $\tau$ between per-tile mIoU and
the PLEM segmentation metrics is 0.47--0.80, and only 0.30 for intersection F1. The gap is starkest
for intersections (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-points}): pixel IoU of the intersection squares is
0.01--0.03 for every model and threshold and does not track detection quality, which ranges from F1
0.001 to 0.279.

SpaceNet6 is the extreme case. mIoU is level (0.477 against 0.480) and both models fail by building
IoU (0.023 against 0.037), yet BF and \texttt{cbhm} are 0.071 against 0.350. The baseline predicts
almost no buildings on SAR (148K pixels over 43 tiles, with 4 tiles empty), while the joint model
predicts buildings on every tile (521K pixels), placed near the ground-truth boundaries but barely
overlapping them. We suspect layover: a side-looking radar places each return by its distance
from the sensor, and a building's roof and upper walls are closer to the sensor than its base, so
in the SAR image they appear shifted toward the sensor, onto the ground in front of the true
footprint. We have not verified this. This is evidence that the joint model finds SAR buildings that pixel
overlap does not credit; it does not show that either model is a good SAR building detector.

\textbf{Loss claim: supported on SpaceNet and SpaceNet6, not on Potsdam.} On SpaceNet the joint
model is better on every PLEM metric and on intersections, with micro F1 0.279 against the
baseline's 0.218 when both thresholds are calibrated on validation tiles (per-tile mean F1
difference $+0.045$, CI $+0.009$ to $+0.081$, $p = 0.015$). The baseline does better on one point
statistic: its probabilities spread wider, so its calibrated threshold gives a predicted/real ratio
near 1 (0.85, against the joint model's 0.39). On Potsdam (buildings only) the PLEM metrics show no
difference (\texttt{cbhm} $-0.004$, CI $-0.037$ to $+0.029$), while the conventional metrics favour
the baseline (building IoU $-0.055$ for the joint model, $p = 0.02$).

\textbf{Darkness.} The joint model degrades less on PLEM's metrics. At severity 0.9, SpaceNet
\texttt{cbhm\_soft} is 0.434 against 0.302, and Potsdam 0.481 against 0.387, although the two
models start level on Potsdam. mIoU shows a much smaller gap on SpaceNet (0.414 against 0.391) and
on Potsdam still favours the baseline at every severity (0.378 against 0.406 at 0.9).

\emph{Caveats.} We trained one seed per model, so the confidence intervals capture variation across
test tiles, not across training runs. The two selection rules differ by design. With ${\sim}28$
paired comparisons, the SpaceNet and SpaceNet6 PLEM-metric differences survive a Bonferroni
correction (threshold 0.0018) except road APLS ($p = 0.0020$); the intersection-F1 and Potsdam IoU
differences do not.
